How the projections work. Clubs only announce starters about ten days out. Past that point each rotation is inferred from who has actually been starting, then rolled forward one slot per game — which keeps it honest through off-days, doubleheaders and postponements. Announced starters always win over the model, and the cycle re-anchors around them. Every club is treated as a five-man rotation except the Dodgers, who run six.

How accurate is it? Backtested over four anchor dates in the 2026 season, the model alone picks the right starter 50–57% of the time in the first week, 34–43% in the second and 30–33% in the third, against 20% for a random guess from a five-man staff. The near columns are mostly announced fact; the far columns are a genuine estimate, and injuries, call-ups and September roster expansion will break them. Treat the far right of the grid as a planning aid, not a promise.
